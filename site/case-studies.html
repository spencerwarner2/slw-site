Verkada

A standing partner through
a decade of scale.

Flex became a standing partner across a decade of scale: enabling Verkada to become a $1B+ bookings company that has continued to successfully raise equity, culminating in a Series F led by CapitalG at a $5.8B valuation. SLW first partnered with the team at Meraki, investing non-dilutive Flex capital ahead of Meraki’s $1.4B acquisition by Cisco. Years later at Verkada, the CEO and board held multiple $1B+ equity offers but wanted to reduce dilution and preserve optionality on their growth plans — so SLW invested $25M in Flex alongside the $96M Series C.

Initial partnership

$25M Flex reduced dilution alongside the Series C, continuing a relationship that began at Meraki.

Future optionality

Standing offers for up to $100M in follow-on Flex funded growth through a market dislocation ahead of the Series E, and let Verkada rethink the timing of its equity financings.

The outcome

A Series F led by CapitalG at a $5.8B valuation — a decade after SLW first partnered with the team at Meraki.

Nex Playground

Fueling a breakout
without pricing a round.

Nex Playground makes a motion-capture, AI computer-vision gaming console. SLW invested $20M in Flex to fund runway ahead of a national retail roll-out and to delay and optimize the company’s next equity round, without setting a new valuation. The company went on to grow revenue 4x year-over-year, reach #1 in Amazon’s Toys & Games category, and raise a $25M Series D at a significant markup.

Initial partnership

$20M Flex funded the retail roll-out and deferred an equity round, preserving optionality on valuation.

Future optionality

Non-dilutive runway let the team hold off pricing a round until performance, not the calendar, set the terms.

The outcome

#1 in Amazon’s Toys & Games category during a record holiday sales season, then a $25M Series D at a significant markup.

Adaptive Insights

Ending dilution,
solving IPO vs. M&A.

After seven prior equity rounds (Series A through G), Adaptive Insights’ board was weighing multiple term sheets while trying to keep timing open to either an IPO or an acquisition. SLW’s Flex — $25M initially, later followed by $15M more — let the company stop diluting and keep adding capital without setting a price, right up through its eventual $1.5B sale to Workday.

Initial partnership

$25M Flex ended further dilution after 7 prior equity rounds, solving for IPO/M&A timing without setting a valuation.

Future optionality

A further $15M funded continued ARR growth and added flexibility to IPO timing.

The outcome

Acquired by Workday for $1.5B, closing out the IPO/M&A question SLW’s capital was built to solve.
